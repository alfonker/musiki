01 · NECK

What changes coming from guitar

Tuned in fifths, low to high G D A E

Same pitches as a violin. Same fret on the next course up is a perfect fifth higher. Fret 7 on any course equals the next course open.

Every shape is fully movable

Guitar has the irregular G–B major third; mandolin has no exception. A shape on G–D moved to D–A or A–E keeps the same fret pattern and the same interval content.

Octave and double-octave shapes

Octave: two courses up, two frets down (G fret 5 = C, A fret 3 = C). Two octaves: three courses up, three frets up (G fret 2 = A3, E fret 5 = A5).

Fretting hand: one finger per two frets

Low positions: index covers frets 1–2, middle 3–4, ring 5–6, pinky 7. Each closed scale box here spans seven frets per course; the index reaches back one fret. You press both strings of a course with one fingertip.

02 · MAJOR & NATURAL MINOR

Major and minor for each root

Pick a root. You get the whole neck to fret 15, three playable positions (open, closed with root on G, closed with root on D) and, below, the closed box for all twelve roots at once. Closed boxes contain no open strings, so they slide to any key.

How to read: fretboard boxes are drawn like tab, highest course (E) on top, fret numbers above. Raspberry dots are roots; the Dots show switch in the bar changes the labels to fingers, degrees or note names.

03 · PENTATONIC

Pentatonic positions on a fifths-tuned neck

On guitar the five CAGED boxes exist because of the tuning: four fourths and one major third force the shape to change as it crosses the B string. On mandolin each course is exactly seven frets above the previous one, so any seven-fret window (index on fret n, pinky on n+6) contains every chromatic pitch exactly once across the four courses. A position is therefore defined only by where your index finger sits.

That gives five positions per pentatonic scale, one for each scale tone placed under the index finger on the G course. Each position covers a little over two octaves (fret n on G to fret n+6 on E). Below they are numbered in order up the neck for the chosen root; position labels say which degree sits under the index.

Minor pentatonic and major pentatonic share the same five boxes (relative keys: A minor pentatonic = C major pentatonic). The blues scale uses the minor pentatonic boxes plus the ♭5.

04 · HARMONIC MINOR & MODES

Harmonic minor, its modes, and the seven church modes

The selector holds the seven modes of the major scale and the seven modes of harmonic minor. Phrygian dominant (5th mode of harmonic minor) is the 12-tone equal-tempered neighbour of makam Hicaz; Hicaz as played on saz uses comma-based intervals that differ from these frets.

05 · CHORDS

Chord library: 12 roots × 9 qualities

Major, minor, maj7, m7, 7, dim, dim7, 6 and m6 for every root. A 6 chord has the same notes as the m7 a minor third below (C6 = Am7), and m6 has the same notes as the m7♭5 a minor third below (Cm6 = Am7♭5). On a four-course mandolin many 6 and m6 shapes have the 6th or 5th on the G course instead of the root, so read those boxes as inversions. Each card shows up to three voicings: one that uses open strings where a good one exists, and closed movable shapes higher up. Seventh-chord voicings may drop the 5th; root, 3rd and 7th are always present. Numbers on dots are suggested fingers.

How to read: chord boxes stand upright with the strings vertical, the leftmost string first in the tuning (G on mandolin, low E on guitar, the high re-entrant G on ukulele, low D on bouzouki). Numbers on dots are suggested fingers, an empty circle above the box is an open string, × a muted one, raspberry dots are roots, and the fret numbers under the box read left to right. Piano diagrams are for two hands: the left keyboard is the left hand (root, plus the fifth when the chord has a perfect fifth), the right keyboard the right hand (the other chord tones in close position; for triads the whole triad). Green keys are chord tones, raspberry keys the root.
